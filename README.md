# SelfIE Replication

This repository contains a replication of Track A from the paper "Learning Self-Interpretation from Interpretability Artifacts: Training Lightweight Adapters on Vector-Label Pairs" (arXiv:2602.10352v2).

## What Was Replicated
We replicated the **Contrastive Topic Vectors** experiment (Section 3.2 of the paper).
The objective is to show that a lightweight trained adapter dramatically improves a frozen language model's ability to self-interpret internal activation vectors (e.g., contrastive vectors for topics) compared to an untrained "SelfIE" / Patchscopes approach.

Specifically, the paper shows that instead of just scaling the vector with a hand-tuned scalar $\alpha$, training a minimal scalar-affine adapter $f(h) = \alpha h + b$ (only $d\_model + 1$ parameters) via cross-entropy loss on target labels significantly improves the model's self-interpretation capability, making it robust to the scaling issue.

### Differences from the Original Paper
- **Model**: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct instead of Llama-3-8B. This was chosen for environmental constraints (faster execution, lower memory overhead, no gated access).
- **Dataset**: A smaller synthetic dataset of ~150 diverse topics, with true labels generated by the model itself, compared to the paper's 49,637 Wikipedia vital articles.
- **Adapter**: We strictly trained the scalar-affine adapter ($d\_model + 1$ params), freezing the entire language model.
- **Layers**: We extract activations from Layer 14 (the middle layer of the 28-layer model) at the final token position.

## Methodology
1. **Dataset Generation**: Generated a list of topics (e.g., "machine learning", "photosynthesis") and used the base LLM to write a 1-sentence description for each.
2. **Extraction**: Computed a contrastive activation vector for each topic by passing `"Tell me about {topic}"`, extracting the residual stream at Layer 14, and subtracting the mean across all topics.
3. **Training**: Split topics 80/20. Trained a scalar-affine adapter $f(h) = \alpha h + b$ using Cross-Entropy on the target labels. We injected the adapted vector into the explanation-seeking prompt: `User: What is the meaning of "Ω"?\nAssistant: The meaning of "Ω" is "`.
4. **Evaluation**: We generated descriptions for the 20% held-out test vectors using both the untrained vector ($h$) and the trained adapter ($f(h)$). We used `sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` to compute embedding similarity against the true labels and report Recall@1 and Recall@10.

## Results

The replication yielded a **null result** under our specific small-scale conditions. Neither untrained SelfIE nor the trained adapter produced coherent topic-specific descriptions. The numbers are far below the paper's reported 82.9% Recall@1.

| Approach | Recall@1 | Recall@10 |
|---|---|---|
| Untrained SelfIE | 3.2% | 19.4% |
| Trained Adapter | 3.2% | 22.6% |
| **Paper (Llama-3-8B, 50k topics)** | **82.9%** | **—** |

### Qualitative Example
**Topic:** "propagating gradients back through a neural network"

- **Untrained Description:** `ISTRY`
- **Trained Description:** `orous`

Neither output bears any relation to backpropagation or gradient computation.

## Diagnostic Investigation

We ran several targeted diagnostics to understand why the pipeline failed, rather than simply attributing it to "small model." The diagnostics below isolate exactly where the signal breaks down.

### 1. Embedding Norm Mismatch

| Vector | L2 Norm |
|---|---|
| Raw contrastive activation (layer 14) | **4.66** |
| Model's token embedding for "the" | **0.78** |

The raw activation vector injected at layer 0 is ~6× larger than a typical token embedding. This is a necessary but not sufficient explanation: even after normalizing the vector to unit norm, the model still failed to produce coherent output (see below).

### 2. Multi-Scale Sweep (Normalized Vectors)

We normalized each contrastive vector to unit L2 norm and swept injection scales on a log grid `[1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32]`. At scale=1, the injected vector has roughly the same magnitude as a real token embedding (~0.78–1.0). Results for 5 held-out topics:

| Topic | Scale 1 | Scale 2 | Scale 4 | Scale 8 | Scale 16 | Scale 32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| the aztec empire | awards | OSTER | OST | OST | OST | OST |
| topology | OST | OST | OST | OST | OST | OST |
| the vikings | OST | OSTERIA | OSTERIA | OST | OST | OST |
| climate | OSTERIA | OSTERIA | OSTERIA | OST | OSTINATO | OSTERIA |
| microeconomics | OST | OST | OST | OST | OST | OST |

**No scale produced any coherent or topic-related text.** This rules out "the vector was just at the wrong magnitude" as the sole explanation. The contrastive vectors extracted at layer 14 of Qwen2.5-1.5B simply do not carry readable topic-specific information when injected at the embedding layer, at any tested scale.

### 3. Adapter Bias Collapse

We injected a **pure zero vector** through the trained adapter — i.e., `adapter(torch.zeros(d_model))`, which outputs just the learned bias `b` — and generated a description:

> **Zero-vector output:** `awards are given to recognize achievements or contributions in various fields.`

This is a coherent English sentence, but it is completely topic-independent. It confirms the adapter collapsed into a **constant mode**: the bias term `b` (norm 15.25) learned to produce a single generic output that minimized average cross-entropy across the training set, while the topic-specific component `alpha * h` was effectively ignored.

### 4. Alpha-Only Isolation (No Bias)

We injected `alpha * h` (skipping the bias entirely) for the same 5 topics:

| Topic | alpha×h norm | Output |
|---|---|---|
| the aztec empire | 4.01 | awards |
| topology | 3.87 | OST |
| the vikings | 3.39 | OSTERIA |
| climate | 2.53 | OSTERIA |
| microeconomics | 3.19 | OST |

These outputs are **identical** to the untrained SelfIE results. The learned alpha (~0.86) barely changed the vector, confirming it carries no interpretable topic signal through this injection pathway in our setup.

### Summary of Failure Mode

The adapter training converged (loss dropped 1.75 → 0.96 over 5 epochs), but what it actually learned was:
- A large bias vector `b` that forces a single coherent-but-generic output sentence
- A near-unity scalar `alpha ≈ 0.86` that does not meaningfully transform the input

The adapter minimized its loss by learning to **ignore the input vector** and **always output a plausible constant**, rather than learning to interpret the vector's topic-specific content.

## Why Our Numbers Differ — Honest Scoping

This is a null result **under our specific small-scale replication conditions**. We tested one layer (14) in one small model (1.5B) with one small dataset (~250 topics). The honest conclusion is that the contrastive topic vectors were not readable in this particular setup, not that the paper's method doesn't work. The key factors:

1. **Model Scale (1.5B vs 8B)**: The paper uses Llama-3-8B. Self-interpretation likely requires a minimum threshold of model capacity to "read" its own internal activations when they are injected at the embedding layer. A 1.5B model may fall below that threshold.

2. **Layer Choice (untested variable)**: We extracted from Layer 14 (exact midpoint of 28 layers). The paper demonstrates that the optimal layer varies across models. We did **not** sweep over layers, so it remains entirely possible that a different layer in Qwen2.5-1.5B would carry readable contrastive signal. This is the single largest untested variable in our replication.

3. **Dataset Size (248 vs 49,637)**: Even with only $d\_model + 1$ parameters, the adapter may need orders of magnitude more training pairs to learn a useful global affine transform instead of collapsing to a constant.

4. **Architecture Mismatch**: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct uses a different architecture (and training data) from Meta's Llama family. The paper's method was developed and validated on Llama models; the degree to which it transfers to other model families is itself an open question.

5. **No Best-of-N Selection**: The paper uses embedding-based candidate selection across multiple generated outputs. We used single-pass greedy decoding.
